An iOS-Photos-style way to crop, straighten and tune a single photo against a live preview of the six-ink e-paper — then submit it straight to the frame. The editing view is fully interactive: drag the crop, pan/zoom, work the sliders, and watch the dither settle.

The editing view

Two modes, iOS-style. Crop first: frame to 4:3 or 3:4, rotate 90°. Then Dither: hokku's pipeline controls organised Lightroom-style — a group bar at rest, tap to slide up that group's sliders — over a live six-ink preview.

Fixed frame, movable photo

The crop frame stays put; you pan and zoom the photo to sit inside it (drag, pinch, wheel, or the zoom slider) — the photo can't pull away from the edges. Rotate 90° either way. Horizontal = a 4:3 landscape frame, Vertical = 3:4 portrait.

One navigator at rest, one editor when active

Lightroom's model: one bottom toolbar of icon buttons — Crop · Tonal · Color · Palette · Kernel — is the single navigator (a porcelain dot marks groups you've edited). Tap a group to bring its sliders into a fixed control bin — a hard four at a time, scroll for the rest; short groups sit at the bottom. The bin height never changes as you switch groups, so the photo stays perfectly still. Sliders are minimalist — a line and a hollow ring — drag anywhere on the row to scrub, double-tap the middle to reset. A changed label + value brighten so you can see what you've touched. Swipe the bin down, or tap the photo, to minimise. The 9-way LUT is a pull-down menu; Preset / Auto / Reset live in the ••• menu.

Starts from Auto

On open, the controls load the server's own best-guess for this photo — its B&W / face detection and matching profile. ↺ Auto (in the ••• menu) returns to it; a tweaked value brightens to porcelain.

Previewed on a photo mat

The image sits on a larger off-white mat — like a matted print — so you can judge brightness the way it'll read against the frame's white ink, not against black. Submit then persists this photo's own crop + settings and converts it once. No "convert" or "preview" button — ever.
